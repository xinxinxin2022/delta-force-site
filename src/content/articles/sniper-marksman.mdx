### Understanding the Numbers

**Damage**: The AWM has the highest damage (115), capable of one-shot kills to the head at any range. The M110 has the highest damage among marksman rifles (55), making it effective for aggressive long-range play.

**Fire Rate**: The AWM is bolt-action with the slowest fire rate, requiring precise shot placement. The marksman rifles are semi-auto, allowing for faster follow-up shots.

**Effective Range**: The AWM has the longest effective range (150m+), while the M110 has the shortest (90m) among these weapons.

**Bullet Velocity**: Higher bullet velocity means less bullet drop and faster time-to-target. The AWM and Mk 14 have very high bullet velocity, making them more forgiving at extreme ranges.

**ADS Speed**: The M110 has the fastest ADS speed, making it more responsive in medium-range engagements. The AWM has the slowest ADS speed, reflecting its role as a precision weapon.

### Weapon-Specific Scope Recommendations

**AWM**:

- **Best Choice**: 6x scope or variable zoom (4-6x)
- **Why**: The AWM's extreme range requires high magnification for precision shots
- **Alternative**: 4x scope if you prefer wider FOV for situational awareness

<Screenshot src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/guoyx/delta-force-site@images-v1/images/awm-sniper-scope-setup.jpg" alt="AWM sniper rifle with 6x scope for long-range precision" />

**Mk 14**:

- **Best Choice**: 3x or 4x scope
- **Why**: The Mk 14's versatility benefits from medium magnification
- **Alternative**: 2x scope for more aggressive, mobile play

**SVD**:

- **Best Choice**: 4x scope or variable zoom (2-4x)
- **Why**: The SVD's long effective range requires good magnification
- **Alternative**: 3x scope if you prefer faster target acquisition

**M110**:

- **Best Choice**: 2x or 3x scope
- **Why**: The M110's faster ADS and shorter range benefit from lower magnification
- **Alternative**: Red dot for aggressive, close-to-medium range play

<Callout type="tip" title="Scope Selection Strategy">
Your scope choice should match your playstyle and the map. High magnification (4x-6x) is ideal for static sniper positions on open maps like Longbow Valley. Lower magnification (2x-3x) is better for mobile combat on mixed maps like Bakhsh.
</Callout>

## Positioning Principles

Regardless of the map, certain positioning principles apply to all long-range combat:

### Height Advantage

**Why it matters**: Elevated positions give you better sightlines, make you harder to hit, and provide psychological advantage.

**How to use it**:

- Always seek elevated positions when possible
- Use buildings, hills, and scaffolding for height advantage
- Be aware that enemies will also try to gain height advantage against you

### Cover and Concealment

**Why it matters**: Cover protects you from enemy fire, while concealment hides your position.

**How to use it**:

- Always position behind hard cover (walls, rocks, buildings)
- Use concealment (bushes, shadows) to hide your position
- Never expose yourself unnecessarily — shoot from cover, then reposition

### Multiple Escape Routes

**Why it matters**: If your position is compromised, you need ways to escape.

**How to use it**:

- Always identify at least two escape routes before engaging
- Keep escape routes clear of obstacles
- Be prepared to abandon your position quickly if flanked

### Sightline Control

**Why it matters**: Controlling key sightlines allows you to dictate engagement terms.

**How to use it**:

- Identify key sightlines on each map (bridges, roads, open areas)
- Position yourself to control these sightlines
- Force enemies to engage on your terms

## When to Use Sniper vs Marksman Rifle

Choosing between a sniper rifle and marksman rifle depends on the situation. Here's a breakdown of when to use each:

### Use Sniper Rifles (AWM) When:

- You're holding a static position with clear sightlines
- You need one-shot elimination capability
- You're on open maps with long sightlines (Longbow Valley)
- You have good cover and multiple escape routes
- You're playing defensively and holding angles

<Callout type="info" title="AWM Positioning">
The AWM is best used from static positions where you can control key sightlines. Avoid using it for mobile combat — the slow ADS and bolt-action fire rate make you vulnerable in dynamic situations.
</Callout>

### Use Marksman Rifles When:

- You need flexibility for mobile combat
- You're engaging at medium-long range (50-100m)
- You're on mixed maps with varied engagement distances (Bakhsh)
- You need faster follow-up shots
- You're playing aggressively and repositioning frequently

### Situational Examples

**Havoc Operations**:

- **Defending extraction**: AWM for holding angles and picking off approaching enemies
- **Rotating between locations**: Mk 14 or M110 for flexible engagements during movement
- **Long-range support**: AWM for providing overwatch for your squad

**All-Out Warfare**:

- **Holding objectives**: AWM for defending key positions
- **Flanking maneuvers**: Mk 14 or M110 for mobile long-range combat
- **Long-range suppression**: SVD for sustained fire at distance

### Quickscoping

Quickscoping is the technique of ADS, firing immediately, and then un-ADS to maintain mobility. This is most effective with marksman rifles:

**How to execute**:

1. ADS quickly
2. Fire immediately when crosshair is on target
3. Un-ADS immediately after firing
4. Reposition or prepare for next shot

**Best weapons**: M110 and Mk 14 (fast ADS speed)

**When to use**: When you need to take a shot quickly without committing to a full ADS

### Drifting and Repositioning

After taking a shot, you need to reposition to avoid counter-fire. Here's how to do it effectively:

**How to execute**:

1. Take your shot
2. Immediately move to a different position (even a few meters)
3. Use cover while repositioning
4. Wait for enemies to reveal themselves before shooting again

**Why it matters**: Staying in the same position after shooting makes you an easy target for counter-fire.
